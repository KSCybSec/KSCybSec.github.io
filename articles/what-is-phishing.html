What Is Phishing?

The message looks real. The sender looks familiar. There's just one problem: someone wants you to make a mistake.

This is phishing

Phishing is a social engineering technique where an attacker impersonates a trusted person, organization, or service to trick someone into taking an action.

That action could be clicking a link, opening an attachment, entering a password, sending money, or revealing information.

Notice something important.

The attacker isn't necessarily breaking through a technical defense. They're trying to convince you to open the door yourself.

What does the attacker want?

CREDENTIALS Usernames and passwords
MONEY Payments or financial information
ACCESS Access to accounts or systems

Sometimes the goal is simply to get malware onto the victim's device.

The technical attack might happen later. The first attack is often against your decision-making.

Phishing isn't always an email

The same idea can appear in different places.

EMAIL A fake message pretending to come from a trusted organization.
SMS A suspicious message asking you to click a link or call a number.
SOCIAL A fake account or message designed to gain trust.
TARGETED A highly personalized phishing attempt aimed at a specific person.

Remember our malware article?

Here's where the two ideas connect.

A phishing message might contain a malicious attachment. Or it might send someone to a website that delivers malware.

Or the attacker may skip malware completely and simply steal the victim's credentials.

So what should you do?

You don't need to become an email detective every time you receive a message.

But when something feels unusual, slow down.

STOP Don't rush because a message says “urgent.”
CHECK Examine the sender, link, request, and context.
VERIFY Use a trusted channel to confirm the request.
REPORT If it's suspicious, report it through the appropriate channel.
A few seconds of skepticism can be more useful than a few seconds of panic.

And this changes how you see email

Before cybersecurity, you might have looked at an email and asked:

“Does this look real?”

Now you can ask better questions.

Think like an investigator

Who sent this? Why did they send it? Where does the link go? What are they asking me to do? Does the situation actually make sense?

That's the mindset we're building throughout this beginner path.

Remember this

Phishing uses deception and trust to convince people to take an action that benefits an attacker. When something feels urgent, unexpected, or unusual, pause and verify before you act.
