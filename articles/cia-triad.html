The CIA Triad

Three simple questions sit underneath many cybersecurity decisions: Who should see the information? Has it been changed? Can we still access it?

A SYSTEM HOLDS SOMETHING IMPORTANT
Hospital Patient Database
Medical records · prescriptions · personal information

What could go wrong?

Imagine you're responsible for protecting the database. A security incident occurs.

THREE DIFFERENT PROBLEMS
CONFIDENTIALITY The wrong person sees the data
?
INTEGRITY The data has been changed
AVAILABILITY The system cannot be accessed

First clue: Confidentiality

Confidentiality means preventing information from being disclosed to people who are not authorized to see it.

Suppose a patient's medical records are accessed by someone who has no permission to view them.

The information may still be accurate. The system may still be working. But confidentiality has been compromised.

Security controls that can help protect confidentiality include:

Encryption — protects data from unauthorized reading.
Authentication — verifies identity.
Authorization — controls access.
Access controls — limit who can reach resources.

Second clue: Integrity

Integrity means keeping information accurate, complete, and protected from unauthorized modification.

A SMALL CHANGE CAN MATTER
10 mg Original medication dosage
→
100 mg Unauthorized modification

The database may still be online. The information may still be accessible. But if important data has been altered without authorization, its integrity has been affected.

Controls that help protect integrity include:

Hashes — help detect changes to data.
Digital signatures — help verify authenticity and integrity.
Audit logs — record important activity.
Change monitoring — helps detect unexpected modifications.

Third clue: Availability

Availability means authorized users can access systems and information when they need them.

THE SYSTEM GOES DOWN
DATABASE Contains the correct information
×
NO ACCESS Users cannot reach the system

A server outage, hardware failure, network problem, or denial-of-service attack can affect availability.

Common measures that support availability include:

Backups — provide recovery options.
Redundancy — provides alternative resources.
Monitoring — helps detect failures.
Failover — can keep services running when one component fails.

Now look at the whole picture.

THE CIA TRIAD
CONFIDENTIALITY Keep information from unauthorized people
+
INTEGRITY Keep information accurate and trustworthy
AVAILABILITY Keep systems and information accessible when needed

One incident can affect all three.

Imagine an attacker compromises a hospital server.

Confidentiality: patient records may be exposed.
Integrity: records could be modified.
Availability: the hospital may lose access to the system.

This is why cybersecurity is not simply about keeping attackers out. Security decisions must consider what information needs protection, whether it can be trusted, and whether systems remain usable.

Think like an investigator.

When something goes wrong, the CIA Triad gives you three useful questions to ask.

INVESTIGATION QUESTIONS
1. Did the wrong person see it?
→ Confidentiality
2. Was the information changed?
→ Integrity
3. Can authorized users still access it?
→ Availability
Important: The CIA Triad is not a rigid checklist where one goal always matters more than the others. Real systems balance security requirements with operational needs.
TAKEAWAY

Protect the data. Protect its accuracy. Keep it available.

Confidentiality protects information from unauthorized disclosure.

Integrity protects information from unauthorized or improper modification.

Availability ensures authorized users can access systems and information when needed.

Together, these three principles form one of the basic foundations for thinking about cybersecurity.
